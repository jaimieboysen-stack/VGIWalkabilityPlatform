Street Segment

1. What is the dominant land use type of this segment?
see more

Decide whether the segment predominantly consists of residential housing or commercial buildings. If your are unsure as the segment is evenly split, choose ‘commercial’.

2. Number of public parks within the segment
see more

A public park should only be counted if they can be accessed along the route walked. Do not count parks beyond the route even if they can be seen from the route

3. Number of public transit stops within the segment
see more

Transit stops located across the street from each other may be counted as ‘1’ stop, as long as they service the same transit line. If the stops are far enough away that a transit user could not quickly run across the street to catch a bus, count ‘2’.

4. Are seating areas or benches present?
see more

Tables or benches outside of restaurants/cafés (see picture) do not count as a places to sit. These must be public seating areas. Transit or bus stop benches are also excluded.

9. Is there a sidewalk present?
see more

A sidewalk need not be nicely paved walking path. As long as it is paved, asphalt or concrete; it will count as a sidewalk. Count any sidewalk along a segment, whether short or long.

10. Are there poorly maintained sidewalk sections that are major tripping hazards?
see more

Major Trip Hazard: An increased likelihood of tripping due to a raising or lowing in the walkway. A hazard could be due to plants, tree roots, or general erosion. A major trip hazard would require pedestrians to look down to avoid tripping.

11. Is there a buffer between sidewalk and road?
see more

Buffer: Separates vehicular and pedestrian zones parallel to the edge of paved roads. They often occupy space between traffic lanes and walking paths that is not intended for either vehicle traffic or walkers. Any buffer on a segment, no matter how long, will be counted.

12. Estimated tree canopy / shade coverage
see more

"Coverage" is the percent of the length of walkway covered by trees, awnings, or other structures providing shade. It need not cover the entire width of the sidewalk. Depending on the time of the year, trees may lose their leaves, so make sure to visualize the trees with their full foliage, in the middle of the day.

How to contribute?

You must be physically present on the street segment in order to begin a survey.

  1. Find a street segment you want to survey on the map
  2. Tap it to select it
  3. Choose between an objective or subjective audit
  4. Fill in the survey and submit your response

What is a street segment?

A street segment is the section of road between two intersections.
